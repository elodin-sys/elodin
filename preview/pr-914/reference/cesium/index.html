Cesium terrain

Use Cesium to run your simulation at a real location, with terrain, aerial imagery or photorealistic 3D scenery streamed into Elodin.

Connect Cesium

In Cesium ion, add the assets you want to use and create a read-only access token for them.

Open the Command Palette, choose Cesium: Connect account, and paste your token.

Choose where to simulate

Choose Scene: Set location in the Command Palette. Enter the latitude, longitude and altitude of your simulation’s origin.

For the Mojave RC field, use 35.350664°, −117.809027°, 589.274 m. Latitude and longitude are in degrees; altitude is above the WGS84 ellipsoid.

Elodin saves this origin with the simulation and uses it for both the scene and ground heights. There is only one location to configure.

Choose what to see

Choose Cesium: Choose scenery in the Command Palette.

SceneryWhat you see
Local terrainYour local scene, without streaming.
Terrain and imageryCesium World Terrain with aerial imagery.
Photo 3DGoogle Photorealistic 3D Tiles, including buildings where available.

Select the assets from your ion account. Photo 3D coverage varies by location.

Run your simulation

Elodin prepares and caches ground heights automatically before the run. Your model reads them during the simulation without waiting for the network. The model defines ground contact; visible Photo 3D buildings do not automatically provide collisions.

Bevy-Cesium streams and renders the scenery as you move. New areas may take a moment to load. Lower the scene detail if you need a smoother view.

How it works

  • One geographic origin. The location you choose anchors local simulation coordinates on Earth. The scene and ground-height queries use the same origin.
  • Scenery streams as you move. Bevy-Cesium loads terrain, imagery or 3D tiles from Cesium ion and renders them in Elodin. It refines the visible area as more detail arrives.
  • Heights are prepared before the run. Elodin’s Python SDK, nox-py, builds a height grid through Bevy-Cesium and caches it. During the simulation, JAX samples that grid locally; a slow connection does not slow down the physics.
  • Your model handles contact. The grid describes the terrain surface. Photo 3D supplies the visible scenery, including buildings, but does not add building collisions to your model.
